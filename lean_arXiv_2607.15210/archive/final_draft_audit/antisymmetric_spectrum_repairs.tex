For $2\leq\ell\leq r$, the coset decomposition of $\mathfrak S_\ell$
over $\mathfrak S_{\ell-1}$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \Pi_\ell=\frac1\ell\left(I-\sum_{j<\ell}F_{j\ell}\right)
        (\Pi_{\ell-1}\otimes I_k).
\end{equation}
Insert this and its adjoint on both sides of $Z\otimes I_k$ and trace
out the last factor. The identity and single-swap terms contribute
$(k-2\ell+2)Z$, while each double-swap term contributes
$R_{\ell-1}^*R_{\ell-1}(Z)$. Hence
\begin{equation}
    R_\ell R_\ell^*
    =\frac{(k-2\ell+2)\,\mathrm{id}
        +(\ell-1)^2R_{\ell-1}^*R_{\ell-1}}{\ell^2}.
\end{equation}
The base case is $R_1R_1^*=k\,\mathrm{id}$. Put
\begin{equation}
    d_j:=\binom kj^2-\binom k{j-1}^2,
    \qquad j=0,\ldots,r,\qquad \binom k{-1}:=0.
\end{equation}
Since $k\geq2r$, the recurrence is strictly positive, so each $R_\ell$
is onto. The nonzero eigenvalues of $R_\ell^*R_\ell$ and
$R_\ell R_\ell^*$ agree, and the remaining kernel has dimension $d_\ell$.
Induction, using
\begin{equation}
    (\ell-1-j)(k-\ell-j+2)+k-2\ell+2
    =(\ell-j)(k-\ell-j+1),
\end{equation}
therefore gives the eigenvalues
$(r-j)(k-r-j+1)/r^2$ of $R_r^*R_r$, with multiplicities $d_j$.
